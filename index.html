1 · What am I looking at?

On an exchange, people place orders: “I will buy XRP at this price” or “I will sell XRP at this price”. Orders wait in a list until someone agrees to trade. That waiting list is called the order book. This page turns it into a moving picture so you can see where lots of buyers and sellers are waiting.

2 · How to read the big picture

  • Left → right = time. The right edge is right now.
  • Up ↑ / down ↓ = price in US dollars. Higher on screen = higher price.
  • Color = how many dollars of orders are waiting at that price (bar below).
  • White line = the actual price over time.
  • Circles = real trades that just happened. Green = a buyer, red = a seller. Bigger = more money.

3 · Why do traders care?

  • A bright stripe below the price = many buyers waiting there. Price often has trouble falling through it. Called a floor (or “support”).
  • A bright stripe above the price = many sellers waiting. Price often has trouble rising through it. Called a ceiling (or “resistance”).
  • Warning: people can cancel orders any second, so these are clues, not promises.

Mini dictionary

XRP: a cryptocurrency. USD: US dollars. Buy order (bid): an offer to buy at a set price. Sell order (ask): an offer to sell at a set price. Spread: the gap between the best buy and best sell price. Wall: a very large order, or stack of orders, at one price. Level 2: all orders added up at each price. Level 3: every single order, one by one. Whale: someone moving a very large amount of money. Liquidity: how much is available to trade at a price. Bid / Ask depth: total money waiting on the buy / sell side. Imbalance: how lopsided buyers vs sellers are. Basis point (bp): 0.01%. Market buy / sell: trading immediately at the best available price. Net flow (CVD): market buys minus market sells. Spoofing: placing big fake orders to fool others, then cancelling them.

5 · What this means for you (small account)

  • Use limit orders, not market orders. A limit order sets the price you accept. Market orders on thin books can fill at a worse price (that loss is called slippage).
  • Small is an advantage. Your order is tiny next to the walls, so you can get in and out quickly without moving the price. Big players cannot.
  • Thin book = fast jumps. When bid or ask depth is low, keep your size smaller and use a stop-loss.
  • Do not trust walls that vanish. If Fast-cancel is high, many big orders are probably fake.
  • Fees matter more for small accounts. Compare your exchange fee plus the spread with what you hope to earn.
  • Your exchange may differ slightly. This shows Kraken and Bitstamp. XRP prices on other exchanges usually follow them closely, but check yours.
  • No chart predicts the future. Only risk money you can afford to lose.
